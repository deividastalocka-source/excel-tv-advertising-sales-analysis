# Hypothesis Testing

## Objective

The purpose of this task was to assess the CEO's claim that the average amount spent on TV advertising by the firm's clients is **£14,000**.

This task was completed by **Aliyah Zaman** as part of the group project.

## Hypotheses

A two-tailed hypothesis test was used.

The hypotheses were:

**H₀: μ = £14,000**

**H₁: μ ≠ £14,000**

The null hypothesis represents the CEO's claim that the population mean TV advertising expenditure is £14,000.

The alternative hypothesis states that the population mean differs from £14,000.

## Sample Statistics

The analysis used:

- **Sample size (n):** 200
- **Sample mean (x̄):** approximately £14,704
- **Sample standard deviation (s):** approximately £8,585
- **Hypothesised population mean (μ₀):** £14,000
- **Degrees of freedom (df):** 199

Because the population standard deviation was unknown, a **one-sample t-test** was used.

## Test Statistic

The test statistic was calculated using:

**t = (x̄ − μ₀) / (s / √n)**

Using the sample values:

**t = (147.04 − 140) / (85.85 / √200)**

The resulting test statistic was approximately:

**t = 1.16**

This represents how many standard errors the observed sample mean is from the hypothesised population mean.

## Critical Value

The analysis used a significance level of:

**α = 0.05**

For a two-tailed test with **199 degrees of freedom**, the critical value used in the project was approximately:

**±1.96**

## Decision

The absolute value of the test statistic was compared with the critical value:

**1.16 < 1.96**

Therefore, the test statistic did not fall within the rejection region.

The analysis therefore **failed to reject the null hypothesis**.

## Interpretation

The sample did not provide sufficient statistical evidence at the 5% significance level to conclude that the true average TV advertising expenditure differs from £14,000.

The result is therefore consistent with the CEO's claim that the firm's clients spend an average of £14,000 on TV advertising.

Failing to reject the null hypothesis does not prove that the true population mean is exactly £14,000. It means that the available sample evidence was not sufficient to conclude that it differs from £14,000.

## Conclusion

The hypothesis test produced a test statistic of approximately **1.16**, which was below the critical value of approximately **1.96**.

The null hypothesis was therefore not rejected, meaning the analysis did not find a statistically significant difference between the observed sample mean and the CEO's claimed population mean of £14,000.
