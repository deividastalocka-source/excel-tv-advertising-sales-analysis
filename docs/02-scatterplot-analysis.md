# Scatterplot Analysis

## Objective

The purpose of this task was to explore the relationship between TV advertising expenditure and subsequent product sales using a scatterplot.

This section represents my individual contribution to the group project.

## Variable Selection

TV advertising expenditure was selected as the **explanatory variable** because it represents the controllable factor that may influence product sales.

Product sales were selected as the **response variable** because sales may change depending on the amount invested in TV advertising.

Therefore:

- **Explanatory variable (x):** TV advertising expenditure
- **Response variable (y):** Product sales

## Creating the Scatterplot

The provided dataset was analysed using Microsoft Excel.

To create the scatterplot:

1. The TV advertising expenditure and sales data were selected.
2. A scatterplot was inserted using Excel.
3. A linear trendline was added to the chart.
4. The regression equation was displayed on the chart.
5. The R² value was displayed on the chart.
6. The axes were labelled to clearly identify the two variables.

![Figure 1 - Scatterplot of TV Advertising Expenditure and Sales](images/Figure%201%20-%20TV%20Advertising%20Scatterplot.png)

*Figure 1. Scatterplot of TV advertising expenditure and product sales with a linear trendline.*

## Trendline

The resulting regression line was:

**ŷ = 0.0475x + 7.0326**

The coefficient of determination displayed on the scatterplot was:

**R² = 0.6119**

The scatterplot shows a positive linear relationship between TV advertising expenditure and product sales.

As expenditure on TV advertising increases, product sales generally increase.

## Interpretation

The positive slope of **0.0475** indicates that for every additional £100 spent on TV advertising, predicted sales increase by approximately **0.0475 thousand**, or **47.5 sales**.

The intercept of **7.0326** indicates that the model predicts approximately **7.0326 thousand sales** when TV advertising expenditure is £0.

The coefficient of determination:

**R² = 0.6119**

indicates that approximately **61.19% of the observed variation in product sales** can be explained by the linear relationship with TV advertising expenditure.

The remaining variation is not explained by TV advertising expenditure within this model and may be associated with other factors.

## Conclusion

The scatterplot provides evidence of a positive linear relationship between TV advertising expenditure and product sales.

Higher expenditure on TV advertising was generally associated with higher sales. The trendline and R² value provided an initial measure of this relationship before the more detailed regression analysis conducted in the next stage of the project.
