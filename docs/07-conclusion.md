# Project Conclusion

## Overall Findings

The project investigated whether there is a linear relationship between TV advertising expenditure and product sales.

The analysis found a positive association between the two variables, with higher TV advertising expenditure generally corresponding with higher product sales.

## Key Results

| Measure | Result |
|---|---:|
| Sample Size | 200 |
| Correlation Coefficient | 0.7822 |
| Coefficient of Determination | 0.6119 |
| Regression Equation | ŷ = 0.0475x + 7.0326 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [134.998, 159.087] |
| 99% Confidence Interval | [131.10055, 162.98445] |
| Hypothesis Test Statistic | ≈ 1.16 |
| Degrees of Freedom | 199 |

## Relationship Between Advertising and Sales

The correlation coefficient of **0.7822** indicated a strong positive linear association between TV advertising expenditure and product sales.

The coefficient of determination:

**R² = 0.6119**

indicated that approximately **61.19% of the observed variation in sales** could be explained by the linear relationship with TV advertising expenditure.

The remaining **38.81%** was not explained by the model and may be associated with other factors affecting sales.

## Regression Model

The regression equation was:

**ŷ = 0.0475x + 7.0326**

The positive slope indicates that increasing TV advertising expenditure is associated with an increase in predicted product sales.

The model can therefore be used to estimate sales at different levels of advertising expenditure, although predictions outside the observed range should be treated with greater caution.

## Model Limitations

Residual analysis identified **heteroscedasticity**, with the variability of the residuals increasing as TV advertising expenditure increased.

This suggests that the simple linear regression model does not fully explain the behaviour of the data.

Other factors not included in the model may also influence product sales, meaning predictions from the regression model should be interpreted with appropriate caution.

## Confidence Intervals

The analysis produced the following confidence intervals for mean TV advertising expenditure:

- **95% confidence interval:** £13,499.80 – £15,908.70
- **99% confidence interval:** £13,110.06 – £16,298.45

The 99% confidence interval was wider because achieving a higher level of confidence requires a larger margin of error.

## CEO Claim

A one-sample t-test was used to assess the CEO's claim that clients spend an average of **£14,000** on TV advertising.

The test statistic was approximately:

**t = 1.16**

This was below the critical value used in the analysis of approximately **±1.96**.

The null hypothesis was therefore not rejected at the 5% significance level. The sample did not provide sufficient evidence to conclude that the true mean TV advertising expenditure differed from £14,000.

## Overall Conclusion

The project provided evidence of a positive linear relationship between TV advertising expenditure and product sales.

TV advertising expenditure explained a substantial proportion of the observed variation in sales. However, the residual analysis demonstrated that advertising expenditure alone does not fully account for sales behaviour.

The project demonstrated how statistical techniques can be used together to analyse a real-world business question and assess both the usefulness and limitations of a statistical model.
